# v1.4.1 — Driving Stability

Crashing the Commodore could throw “Invalid typed array length” because panel subdivision increased the position count without increasing vertex colours. Subdivision now interpolates RGBA alongside positions and UVs. A shared buffer check repairs already-stale float channels before geometry cloning or crash reads. Repeated impacts and repairs retain valid buffer counts and the original imported surface normals.

Imported Life Models show stronger local folds and shading, with the visual contact moved onto the nearest actual surface when a broad aircraft/car hitbox lies outside its skin. Deformation reuses updatable GPU buffers, touches affected vertices, publishes at 20–30 Hz and stops once settled. Existing limits on active cages and detached parts remain. This is the game's bounded node/beam approximation, not a manufacturer-calibrated full soft-body solver.

Entering a borrowed imported car builds a complete high-detail player body/interior, copies its appearance and crash history, and binds suspension/lights to its new live nodes. It no longer depends on the low-detail NPC body's visibility or disposed temporary wheels. Player imports keep their full source interior even in Low mode; NPC/remote imports remain lower detail. Glass opacity, interior alpha handling, dark interior finishes and repair normals are corrected. Balanced and higher modes share a small mipmapped sky-reflection texture rather than rendering a cube camera per car. Imported source lamp materials illuminate in place of floating generic lamp boxes; exact lamp separation depends on the supplied asset materials.

Seven imported road cars have individually aligned eyes and seats, stable cockpit-height compensation and a clear forward view over the dashboard. The driver sits on the matching side and is scaled to the cabin, including below the Porsche roof. Interior fidelity still depends on the supplied mesh/textures; missing source switches or instruments are not reconstructed as calibrated manufacturer equipment.

Walking defaults to facing and following actual travel direction. Holding the mouse allows free look; releasing recentres the view. Direction inputs latch their movement basis, so holding a strafe key does not make the camera and character circle indefinitely.

Ground-vehicle building streaming prepares batched mapped-footprint wall/roof shells in 512 m cells ahead of motion, then swaps in richer local scenery. Source colours and footprint holes are retained. Matching shell triangles are masked once detailed geometry exists, avoiding duplicate house skins. Cells are built incrementally, collision indexed and evicted outside the reserve. The test neighbourhood produced 294 houses across seven cells in fourteen batches without duplicate covered ranges. These shells improve visibility while detail catches up; they cannot create surveyed house data where the map has none, and are not an unconditional load-time/FPS guarantee.

Cars and bikes now enter mapped water instead of stopping at an invisible wall. They lose momentum, take on water, sink and flood the engine. Dropping from a bridge falls to the water first. A submerged driver has a finite air supply and drowns if they remain inside. Press **X** to leave a car (**H** on bikes); the person exits at the actual submerged door and swims upward before heading to shore. Remaining air carries over on exit. Recover/garage cannot bypass escape while trapped inside. Abandoned and NPC vehicles continue sinking. Where the DEM lacks underwater soundings, seabed depth is a bounded gameplay estimate rather than surveyed bathymetry. Boats retain their existing handling.

Verification: Chrome tests reproduce the stale-colour-buffer failure and repeated Commodore crash/repair cycles; cover seven car interiors, high-detail hijack bodies, repaired normals, driver fit, working wheel bindings and hidden generic lamp boxes; exercise walking/camera recentering and ahead-of-car footprint shells; and test real mapped water entry, flooding, engine failure, blocked recovery, NPC sinking, escape, surfacing and drowning. The nine-model suite additionally exercises rescue rotor/fighter gear and deformation on all imported vehicle types. Crash-identity/pileup and damage-model regressions are retained. Tutorial/help and download instructions include the new water behaviour.

Earlier unfinished requests remain in [TASKS.md](../TASKS.md). The combat/fleet expansion moves to the next bundle so this stability release can address the reported failures first.
